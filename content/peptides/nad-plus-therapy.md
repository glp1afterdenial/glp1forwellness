<p class="section-label">The basics</p>

## What NAD+ Actually Is {#what-is-nad}

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is not exotic. It's one of the most abundant molecules in your body — a coenzyme found in every living cell, required for:

- **Energy production** — NAD+ shuttles electrons in the mitochondrial reactions that convert food into ATP, your cellular fuel
- **DNA repair** — PARP enzymes, which fix damaged DNA, consume NAD+ every time they run
- **Cellular stress response** — sirtuins, the "longevity enzymes" activated by exercise and fasting, can't function without NAD+
- **Circadian rhythm regulation** — NAD+ levels cycle daily and help set your metabolic clock

The reason anyone cares: **NAD+ declines with age.** Estimates vary by tissue, but studies suggest levels may fall by roughly half between your 40s and 60s. Chronic inflammation, overeating, alcohol, and poor sleep accelerate the decline. Lower NAD+ means mitochondria work less efficiently and repair enzymes run short on fuel — a plausible contributor to the fatigue, slower recovery, and metabolic drift of aging.

<p class="section-label">The research</p>

## What the Research Shows (and Doesn't) {#evidence}

**Well-established:**

- Oral NAD+ precursors (nicotinamide riboside, NMN) **reliably raise blood NAD+ levels** in randomized human trials — typically 40-100%+ increases
- Short-term human safety at studied doses is good
- In animal models, NAD+ restoration improves mitochondrial function, insulin sensitivity, and markers of cellular aging

**Promising but early:**

- Small human trials suggest possible benefits for blood pressure, arterial stiffness, muscle recovery, and insulin sensitivity in specific populations
- An NMN trial in prediabetic women showed improved muscle insulin sensitivity — one of the more cited human results
- Research on NAD+ for cognition, chronic fatigue, and addiction recovery is ongoing

**Not proven:**

- No large trial has shown NAD+ therapy extends human lifespan or prevents age-related disease
- Most dramatic "anti-aging" results come from mice, not people
- IV infusion marketing often outruns the evidence considerably

<div class="callout callout--red">

**Honest framing:** raising NAD+ levels is proven and safe short-term. Whether raised NAD+ translates into feeling and aging meaningfully better is where the evidence is still maturing. Many users report real energy and recovery benefits; placebo-controlled data is thinner than the hype suggests. Anyone promising you'll "reverse aging" is selling, not citing.

</div>

## Injection vs Nasal Spray vs Tablets {#formats}

NAD+ therapy comes in three practical formats (IV infusions being the expensive clinic-only fourth):

**Subcutaneous injection**
- Small needle, similar to a GLP-1 pen — delivers NAD+ directly, bypassing digestion
- Preferred by users who want the strongest effect short of IV
- Typical protocol: 1-2x weekly

**Nasal spray**
- Needle-free, absorbed through nasal mucosa
- Convenient for daily or as-needed use; some users like it for mid-day energy
- Absorption is faster than oral, less complete than injection

- Daily dosing builds levels gradually

## NAD+ and GLP-1s: Why People Combine Them {#glp1}

This site exists because of GLP-1s, so the natural question: does NAD+ fit alongside semaglutide or tirzepatide?

The rationale people use:

- **Fatigue is a top GLP-1 complaint** — eating substantially less means less incoming fuel, and NAD+ sits at the center of converting fuel to energy
- **Muscle preservation matters during weight loss** — NAD+ supports mitochondrial function in muscle; some users add it during aggressive weight-loss phases alongside protein and resistance training
- **Metabolic synergy is plausible** — GLP-1s improve insulin sensitivity from the hormone side; NAD+ supports the cellular machinery underneath

To be clear: **no clinical trial has tested the GLP-1 + NAD+ combination.** The rationale is mechanistic, the reports anecdotal. There are no known interactions, and both are prescribed together routinely by longevity-focused telehealth providers — but this is informed experimentation, not established protocol.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

<div class="callout">

**Will I feel NAD+ working?**

Not like caffeine. Users who respond describe steadier energy, better sleep quality, and faster recovery emerging over 2-6 weeks. Some feel nothing subjective — blood levels still rise, but perceptible benefit varies person to person.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Why not just take cheap niacin? It's also vitamin B3.**

Niacin does raise NAD+ but causes flushing at meaningful doses and doesn't match the precursor pathways (NR/NMN) used in trials. Direct NAD+ injection or spray bypasses conversion entirely. Different tools, different tolerability.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Are NAD+ injections painful?**

They use small subcutaneous needles like insulin or GLP-1 pens — most people rate them minor. Some report brief warmth or flushing after injection, which fades. IV infusions are the format notorious for discomfort, another reason subcutaneous won out.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Can I boost NAD+ without supplements?**

Yes, partially: exercise (especially zone 2 and resistance training), time-restricted eating, good sleep, and limiting alcohol all support NAD+ metabolism. Supplementation stacks on top of those, not instead of them.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Is compounded NAD+ FDA-approved?**

No. NAD+ therapies are compounded by licensed U.S. pharmacies or sold as supplements; they have not been evaluated by the FDA for safety or effectiveness. This is standard for the category — it's why going through a licensed provider who screens your health history matters.

</div>
