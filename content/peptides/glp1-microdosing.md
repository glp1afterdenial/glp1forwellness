The standard GLP-1 narrative goes: start low, titrate up every 4 weeks, push to the maximum tolerated dose, lose 15-20% of body weight. It's effective. It also makes a lot of people miserable — nausea, vomiting, constipation, and fatigue are dose-dependent, and some people simply can't tolerate high doses.

Enter microdosing: the recognition that the "right" dose isn't always the highest one.

---

<h2 id="what-is-it" style="border-left: 4px solid #d97706; padding-left: 16px; color: #1c1917;">What GLP-1 Microdosing Means</h2>

There's no official clinical definition of "microdosing" a GLP-1. In practice, it means:

**Lower starting doses:**
- Semaglutide: 0.125-0.25mg weekly (vs standard 0.25mg start → 2.4mg target)
- Tirzepatide: 1.25-2.5mg weekly (vs standard 2.5mg start → 15mg target)

**Slower titration:**
- Advancing every 6-8 weeks instead of every 4
- Smaller jumps between doses
- Stopping the titration once appetite control is achieved, regardless of whether you've reached "maximum dose"

**Low-dose maintenance:**
- Staying at 0.25-1mg semaglutide long-term
- Using 2.5-5mg tirzepatide as a maintenance dose after weight loss
- Prioritizing tolerability and sustainability over maximum effect

The philosophy: **find your minimum effective dose** — the amount that controls appetite and supports metabolic health without making you feel lousy.

<h2 id="who-its-for" style="border-left: 4px solid #d97706; padding-left: 16px; color: #1c1917;">Who GLP-1 Microdosing Is For</h2>

**The side-effect sensitive**
- People who've tried standard doses and couldn't tolerate the nausea or GI symptoms
- Those with gastroparesis risk or already-slow digestion
- Anyone who experienced vomiting, severe constipation, or couldn't eat at higher doses

**The modest-goal group**
- Only need 10-20 lbs of loss
- Want metabolic health benefits (A1c, lipids, blood pressure) more than dramatic weight loss
- Seeking appetite awareness, not appetite obliteration

**Post-loss maintenance**
- Already lost significant weight on full-dose GLP-1
- Want to keep weight off without ongoing high-dose side effects
- Looking for the minimum dose that prevents regain

**The cautious starter**
- First time trying a GLP-1 and want to ease in
- Worried about side effects based on others' experiences
- Prefer a "start low and slow" approach

<h2 id="evidence" style="border-left: 4px solid #d97706; padding-left: 16px; color: #1c1917;">What the Research Shows</h2>

GLP-1 effects are **dose-dependent** — this is established:

- **STEP 1-4 trials:** 2.4mg semaglutide → ~15% weight loss; lower doses in the SUSTAIN trials (0.5mg, 1.0mg) still produced 3.5-6.5% body weight loss
- **SURMOUNT trials:** 15mg tirzepatide → ~21% loss; 5mg still produced ~15% — suggesting the dose-response curve isn't linear (you get a lot from moderate doses)
- **Cardiovascular benefits:** The SELECT trial used 2.4mg, but prior diabetes trials at 0.5-1mg semaglutide showed improved cardiovascular markers

Key insight from the data: **you don't need maximum dose for meaningful benefit.** The first 5-10% of weight loss produces most of the metabolic health improvement (blood pressure, A1c, lipids, liver fat). If that's achievable at a dose you can tolerate indefinitely, higher dosing may be unnecessary for your goals.

<div style="background: #fef2f2; border: 1px solid #fecaca; border-radius: 12px; padding: 24px; margin: 20px 0;">

**Honest limitation:** no trial has specifically studied "microdosing" protocols head-to-head against standard protocols for long-term outcomes. The evidence supports low-dose efficacy, but optimal microdosing schedules are based on clinical experience and extrapolation, not dedicated RCTs. Your provider is working from pharmacology and pattern recognition, not a microdosing-specific trial.

<h2 id="protocols" style="border-left: 4px solid #d97706; padding-left: 16px; color: #1c1917;">Common Microdosing Protocols</h2>

**Gentle ramp (for sensitive starters):**
- Semaglutide: 0.125mg × 4-6 weeks → 0.25mg × 4-6 weeks → hold or advance only if needed
- Goal: minimize the "first dose nausea" that makes many people quit early

**Minimum effective dose (maintenance):**
- Find the lowest dose that controls appetite meaningfully
- Typically 0.25-0.5mg semaglutide or 2.5-5mg tirzepatide
- Hold there indefinitely, adjusting only if appetite regulation fades

**Step-down after weight loss:**
- Lose weight at standard dose → step down to microdose for maintenance
- Many providers halve the dose (or more) once goal weight is reached
- Monitors: if weight starts creeping, the dose can be bumped back up

**Compounding advantage:** compounded semaglutide can be dosed in precise increments (0.125mg, 0.175mg, etc.) — branded pens only come in fixed doses. This makes microdosing much more practical with compounding pharmacies.
