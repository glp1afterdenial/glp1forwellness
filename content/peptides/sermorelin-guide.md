Growth hormone has a perception problem. For most people it conjures images of bodybuilders and banned substances. But GH is a normal, essential hormone that declines steadily with age — and its loss contributes to the muscle wasting, stubborn fat, poor sleep, and slow recovery that creep in after 30.

Sermorelin offers a way to push GH back up without the legal, cost, and safety issues of synthetic HGH. Here's the practical guide.

<p class="section-label">The basics</p>

## What Sermorelin Is {#what-is-sermorelin}

Sermorelin is a synthetic analogue of GHRH — growth hormone-releasing hormone. Your hypothalamus naturally produces GHRH to tell the pituitary gland "make GH now." Sermorelin mimics that signal.

The result: your pituitary releases growth hormone in its normal pulsatile pattern (mostly during deep sleep), just *more* of it. This is fundamentally different from injecting the hormone itself, because:

- Your feedback loops stay intact — the pituitary won't produce dangerously high levels because it still self-regulates
- GH pulses naturally, especially overnight — sermorelin amplifies the signal without flatlining it
- IGF-1 (the downstream marker) rises gradually, not abruptly

Sermorelin was originally FDA-approved (as Geref Diagnostic) and used in pediatric endocrinology. It was discontinued in 2008 for commercial reasons — not safety concerns. Today it's prescribed off-label by longevity and wellness providers as a compounded peptide.

## Injection vs Tablet vs Lozenge {#formats}

**Subcutaneous injection** (most common)
- Small insulin-style needle, typically injected before bed (to align with natural GH pulse)
- Fastest onset, most established delivery method
- 5-7 nights per week depending on protocol

**Oral tablet**
- Available from some compounding pharmacies and telehealth programs
- More convenient, no needles, easier for travel
- Absorption through the gut is less efficient than injection — doses are adjusted accordingly
- Some providers use tablets as a maintenance option after establishing results with injection

**Oral lozenge (troche)**
- Dissolves in the mouth, absorbing partly through the oral mucosa — bypasses some of the gut-absorption loss of swallowed tablets
- Typical protocol: once daily for 5 days on, 2 days off

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

<div class="callout">

**Can I take sermorelin with a GLP-1 medication?**

Yes. There are no known interactions, and the combination is common — GLP-1s handle appetite and weight loss while sermorelin supports lean muscle preservation and recovery. Many longevity-focused telehealth programs offer both.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Will sermorelin show up on a drug test?**

Peptides like sermorelin can be detected on sophisticated sports anti-doping tests. For standard employment drug screens, no — they don't test for peptides. If you're a competitive athlete in a WADA-tested sport, sermorelin is prohibited. For general wellness use, not an issue.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**Why inject at night?**

Growth hormone naturally surges during deep sleep. Injecting sermorelin before bed (typically 30-60 minutes prior) aligns with this rhythm, amplifying the natural nighttime pulse rather than forcing an unnatural daytime one. Some protocols also recommend an empty stomach.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**What side effects should I expect?**

Most common: injection site redness, occasional headache, flushing, or dizziness — usually mild and transient. Some users report vivid dreams or increased appetite early on. Joint pain or water retention (common with synthetic HGH) are uncommon with sermorelin because levels stay within physiological range.

</div>

<div class="callout">

**How do I know if it's working?**

Track sleep quality first — it's the earliest signal. After 2-3 months, body composition changes should be measurable (waist circumference, body fat). If your provider does labs, IGF-1 should rise. If nothing changes after 4-6 months of consistent use, dosing may need adjustment or it may not be your limiting factor.

</div>
