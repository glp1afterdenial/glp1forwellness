<p class="section-label">The concern</p>

## Why Weight Loss Affects Bones {#weight-loss-bone}

Bone density and body weight are closely linked. Mechanical loading from body weight stimulates bone formation — it's why weight-bearing exercise builds bone. When you lose significant weight through **any method**, bone mineral density (BMD) typically decreases:

- **Bariatric surgery** — hip BMD drops 5-10% in the first year (the most bone loss of any method)
- **Caloric restriction** — moderate BMD reductions proportional to weight lost
- **GLP-1 medications** — some studies report 2-7% hip BMD reduction during active weight loss

This isn't unique to GLP-1s — it's a biophysical consequence of carrying less weight. The important question is whether GLP-1 medications cause **more** bone loss than expected for the weight lost, or less.

<p class="section-label">The evidence</p>

## What the Research Actually Shows {#what-research-shows}

### Fracture risk: the most important outcome

Bone density is a surrogate marker. What actually matters is whether people break bones. The data here is reassuring:

- **SELECT trial** (17,604 participants, semaglutide 2.4 mg vs placebo): no significant increase in fracture rates despite average 15% weight loss
- **SUSTAIN and STEP programs**: pooled analyses show no signal for increased fractures across thousands of patients
- **Observational studies**: some large database studies actually show **reduced fracture risk** in GLP-1 users compared to matched non-users — potentially because of reduced falls from better mobility and reduced inflammation

### Bone density changes

A 2024 analysis from the STEP program found that semaglutide users did lose some hip BMD, but **less than predicted** for the amount of weight lost. This suggests GLP-1 medications may have a protective effect on bone beyond their weight loss impact.

The mechanism is biologically plausible: GLP-1 receptors are present on **osteoblasts** (bone-forming cells), and GLP-1 signaling appears to promote bone formation while inhibiting bone resorption. GIP receptors — relevant for tirzepatide — also exist on bone cells and may provide additional skeletal protection.

### GLP-1s vs surgery for bone impact

Bariatric surgery causes substantially more bone loss than GLP-1 medications for equivalent weight loss. This is partly because surgery alters calcium absorption and nutrient status. GLP-1 medications preserve the GI tract and normal nutrient absorption, making them a **more bone-friendly route** to significant weight loss.

## How to Protect Your Bones on GLP-1 Therapy {#protecting-bones}

<div class="callout callout--green">

**Four evidence-based bone protection strategies:**

- **Resistance training 2-3x per week** — muscle loading directly stimulates bone formation and is the single most important intervention for bone health during weight loss
- **Adequate protein (1.0-1.2 g/kg/day)** — preserves muscle mass, which mechanically supports bone; higher intake recommended during active weight loss
- **Calcium (1,000-1,200 mg/day) + Vitamin D (1,000-2,000 IU/day)** — the foundation of bone maintenance; get vitamin D levels checked
- **DEXA scan baseline** — if you're over 50, postmenopausal, or have risk factors, get a DEXA scan before starting treatment and repeat at 1-2 years

</div>

The single biggest mistake: extreme caloric restriction stacked on top of GLP-1 appetite suppression. Eating too little accelerates muscle and bone loss far beyond what the medication alone causes. Aim for moderate caloric deficit with high protein, not crash dieting.

## What We Don't Know Yet {#caveats}

<div class="callout callout--amber">

**Important limitations:**

- **Long-term data is limited** — most trials followed patients for 1-2 years; 5-10 year bone effects are unknown
- **Dose matters** — higher doses causing more weight loss may have proportionally greater bone effects
- **Population differences** — postmenopausal women and older adults may be more vulnerable to bone loss; most trials had mixed populations
- **Stopping effects** — weight regain after stopping GLP-1s may restore bone density, but this hasn't been rigorously studied
- **Individual variation** — some people may lose more bone than others depending on genetics, baseline density, and lifestyle factors

</div>

<p class="section-label">FAQ</p>

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

**Do GLP-1 medications cause bone loss?**

The picture is nuanced. Any significant weight loss reduces bone mineral density — this happens with surgery, diet, and medications alike. Some studies show GLP-1 users lose 2-7% of hip bone density during treatment. However, GLP-1 medications may preserve bone better than equivalent weight loss from other methods, because they reduce inflammation and may have direct protective effects on bone cells through GLP-1 receptors on osteoblasts.

**Do GLP-1 medications increase fracture risk?**

Large clinical trials have not found increased fracture rates with semaglutide or tirzepatide. The SELECT trial (17,604 participants) and SUSTAIN/STEP programs showed no significant increase in fractures despite weight loss. Some observational studies actually suggest reduced fracture risk in GLP-1 users compared to non-users. The concern exists in theory because of bone density changes, but the clinical fracture data has been reassuring.

**Is semaglutide or tirzepatide safer for bone health?**

Both appear relatively safe for bone health based on available clinical trial data. Some studies suggest semaglutide may have slightly more favorable bone markers compared to tirzepatide, but head-to-head comparisons are limited. The dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism of tirzepatide may offer bone benefits since GIP receptors are found on bone cells. Neither has shown clinically meaningful increases in fracture risk in trials.

**Should I take calcium and vitamin D while on a GLP-1 medication?**

Yes — this is standard guidance for anyone undergoing significant weight loss. Adequate calcium (1,000-1,200 mg/day) and vitamin D (1,000-2,000 IU/day) help preserve bone during weight loss. Resistance training is equally important because muscle loading stimulates bone formation. Ask your provider about a baseline DEXA scan if you have osteoporosis risk factors.

**Are GLP-1 medications safe for people who already have osteoporosis?**

GLP-1 medications are not contraindicated in osteoporosis. If you have osteoporosis and need weight management or diabetes treatment, GLP-1s remain a reasonable option — but proactive bone protection is important. Work with your provider on DEXA monitoring, calcium/vitamin D supplementation, resistance exercise, and potentially concurrent osteoporosis medication if indicated.

**How can I protect my bones while taking GLP-1 medications?**

Four evidence-based strategies: resistance training 2-3 times per week (muscle loading directly stimulates bone formation), adequate protein intake (1.0-1.2 g/kg/day to preserve muscle and bone), calcium and vitamin D supplementation, and monitoring with DEXA scans if you have risk factors. Avoid crash dieting or extreme caloric restriction, which accelerates bone loss beyond what the medication causes.
